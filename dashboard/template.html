Worth the fee?

Every player Barça paid a transfer fee for since 2010, judged on what he did at the club (measured against players in the same role, with extra credit for trophies he helped win) and on the money Barça got back when he left. It also asks whether each fee was reasonable at the moment of signing, and how Barça's record compares with Real Madrid's and Atlético's. How the verdicts are made

Role-aware season scores

Each season from 2012/13 on is scored against players in the same role. La Liga: goals and assists per 90, share of team minutes, team points with and without him, goals conceded while he plays. Europe: goals and assists per 90 and share of minutes against the Champions League or Europa League field, with Europa League output discounted.

Market model

A linear regression on La Liga player-seasons learns what the market pays for that output, adjusted for age, team strength and Champions League football (R² ). Each Barça season gets a performance-implied value. Fair fee = that value × , the typical premium over Transfermarkt values.

Deal balance (headline)

Performance value + money back − fee. Each season is credited with 1/ of its fair fee, priced as if the player were 27, so a season is worth the same whatever his age. Money back is the sale fee, loan fees, or current value if he's still at the club. A fair-priced player who plays at that level for five seasons and leaves for free comes out at €0. The verdict is profit or loss only when the whole 80% range sits on one side of zero.

Trophy points

For every competition Barça won, a player earns points for his part in it: 60% share of the team's minutes, 40% share of its goals and assists. Weights: Champions League 3, La Liga 2, Copa del Rey and Europa League 1, Club World Cup 0.75, Supercopa and UEFA Super Cup 0.5. The market model then learns what a point is worth: each point adds about % to a season's value, so a leading role in a treble (Neymar in 2014/15 earned 3.6 points) lifts that season's value by about ×.

Decision at signing

What was he worth when Barça signed him? The same kind of market model, fitted on 92,000 player-seasons across 14 leagues (with league strength built in), is applied to his last two league seasons before joining. That's cross-checked against his Transfermarkt value on the day. A fee counts as overpaid at signing only when both views say so, since stats miss potential and valuations can run hot.

Two supporting tests

Fee test: fee vs fair fee, from performance alone. Total cost: fee + wages − money back, compared with value delivered, benchmarked on Barça's other signings. When the deal balance is inconclusive but both tests agree, the headline leans their way.

Money: Today's football € uses the average value of the 200 most valuable top-five-league players (prices roughly tripled from 2010 to 2025). Consumer prices uses approximate euro-area HICP inflation. Actual € applies no adjustment. The football index fits the data best: the total-cost model explains of the variation with it, against in actual euros. Limitations: box-score data misses build-up and pressing work, 2010–12 seasons have no appearance data, and wages come from Capology: actual player salaries for Barça in 2017/18, 2018/19 and 2020/21; otherwise each club's payroll for the season, split across the squad by market value and age. Source: transfermarkt-datasets (snapshot to July 2026), Capology payrolls, and hand-curated fees for early deals missing from the dataset.
